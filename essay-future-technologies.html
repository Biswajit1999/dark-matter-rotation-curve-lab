Author perspective · engineering gates

A 50-year technology horizon without science fiction

I use a 50-year horizon as a planning thought experiment, not as a claim that dark-matter technology will exist on schedule. Every capability is conditional on experimentally crossing the previous physics gate.

First-person research synthesisEvidence ≠ speculationUpdated 28 September 2026

The gate sequence

identity → coupling → control → capture/confinement → energy transfer → directed momentum → engineering system

Skipping an arrow produces a story, not a technology programme. I therefore score concepts by which gate has actually been demonstrated.

Navigation and field mapping

If a stable local dark-sector signal became measurable, one long-horizon application could be a new inertial or environmental reference. Streams or coherent fields might complement stellar, pulsar or atomic navigation—but only if the signal is stable, mapped and distinguishable from instrument drift.

Energy: abundance is not availability

ṁ/A = ρv
P_kin/A = ½ρv³
P_rest/A = η_capture η_conversion ρvc²

The rest-energy expression is an upper ceiling, not a mechanism. If interaction probability is tiny, a huge collector remains almost transparent. Any honest energy concept must include capture, conversion, heat rejection and system mass.

Propulsion: close the momentum ledger

F/A ≤ ε_p ρv²
Δv = (F/M_spacecraft) Δt

Momentum conservation is not optional. A dark-matter engine would need a directional momentum-exchange channel. Present evidence supplies no reactionless-drive loophole, gravity shield or faster-than-light mechanism.

What I would fund over the next decades

I would fund lower thresholds, directional detection, quantum sensing, better halo phase-space maps, precision lensing, public calibration products and cross-experiment replication. Those investments remain useful whether the answer is a WIMP, axion, ultralight field, hidden sector, modified gravity or something not yet named.

My long-horizon view

The scientific leap is not to imagine the most spectacular engine. It is to discover one new controllable physical property and then exhaust its consequences rigorously. A century of technology can grow from a small repeatable effect; no amount of visual futurism can substitute for that first effect.
