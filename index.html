In simple words

You do not need a cosmology textbook to enter the project. These two ideas are related only because both are still physically unexplained.

Dark matter

Mass we cannot see with light.

Galaxies and clusters behave as if extra gravitating mass is present. We infer it from motion, lensing and cosmic structure—but we still do not know what it is made of.

We can measure
its gravitational effects
We still do not know
its microscopic identity
Dark energy

The name for accelerated cosmic expansion.

The universe is not only expanding; its expansion is accelerating. Dark energy is the label for whatever drives that late-time behaviour in our models.

We can measure
the expansion history
We still do not know
the underlying physics

Observed tracer

What telescopes measure

Optical images locate galaxies, X-ray photons trace hot plasma and coherent distortions appear in background-galaxy shapes.

Reconstructed quantity

What lensing infers

Shape distortions constrain projected gravitational potential. Recovering a convergence or mass map requires source redshifts, calibration and inversion assumptions.

Discriminating pattern

Why collisions matter

Gas experiences ram pressure; stars are nearly collisionless. When most reconstructed mass follows galaxies instead of gas, the dominant gravitating component is not the plasma.

Independent baryon anchor

Why “all matter is baryonic” fails

Primordial nucleosynthesis and CMB acoustic structure constrain the baryon density. Replacing Ωcdm with baryons changes photon–baryon oscillations instead of reproducing the same sky.

Growth mechanism

Why early clustering matters

Before recombination, baryons are coupled to photons. A non-baryonic component can begin building gravitational wells without participating in that acoustic fluid.

DESI · updated July 2026

Evolving dark energy remains an open test

DESI DR2 full-shape Lyα measurements moved toward the Planck ΛCDM prediction. The site therefore treats w₀–wₐ exploration as a competing-model exercise, not a discovery of evolving dark energy.

Hypothesis laboratory

Is dark matter hidden in another dimension?

No extra spatial dimension is required for matter to be electromagnetically dark. A particle in ordinary 3+1-dimensional spacetime can simply lack a useful electromagnetic coupling. Extra-dimensional models are separate, testable beyond-Standard-Model hypotheses.

baseline physics

3 spatial dimensions + time

Weak or absent electromagnetic coupling is enough to prevent ordinary imaging. Gravity can still reveal the mass distribution.

Capability roadmap · 2026–2076

What the next 50 years can actually improve

This is a staged research pathway, not a promise that discovery will arrive on a schedule. Each stage has a measurable validation gate.

  1. 2026–2035

    Join the sky surveys

    Cross-calibrate Rubin, Euclid and Roman lensing maps; expand resolved galaxy dynamics; keep tonne-scale rare-event detectors accumulating exposure.

    Gate: consistent halo structure across instruments, wavelengths and analysis pipelines.
  2. 2035–2050

    Turn backgrounds into measurements

    Scale ultra-low-background detectors, directional recoil sensing, cryogenic thresholds and axion-band coverage while combining CMB lensing with galaxy surveys.

    Gate: a repeatable signal with target, direction or annual-modulation tests—or stronger exclusions that retire model families.
  3. 2050–2076

    Build for the surviving parameter space

    Condition future observatories on what earlier null results leave open: quantum sensors, interferometric concepts, distributed timing and next-generation underground targets.

    Gate: independent replication plus a shared astrophysical and laboratory explanation. Mission names and architectures remain conditional.
