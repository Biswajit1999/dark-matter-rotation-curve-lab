Author perspective · galaxy dynamics

What galaxy rotation curves actually tell us

A flat rotation curve is visually simple, but the inference beneath it is not. I read a rotation curve as a competition between measured kinematics, a baryonic mass model, geometry and a gravitational hypothesis.

First-person research synthesisEvidence ≠ speculationUpdated 28 September 2026

Why this matters

Galaxies give us thousands of resolved measurements rather than one cosmological number. That makes them laboratories for the radial structure of the mass discrepancy and for the degeneracies that a one-number summary would hide.

Physical intuition

For circular motion, more enclosed gravitating mass means a larger circular speed. If the luminous contribution becomes centrally concentrated while the measured speed remains high at large radius, then either additional gravitating structure is present or the mapping from baryons to acceleration needs modification.

Equations I keep visible

v_model² = v_gas² + Υ_disk v_disk² + Υ_bul v_bul² + v_halo²
χ² = Σ[(v_obs − v_model)/σ]²
g_obs = v_obs²/r    g_bar = v_bar²/r

The stellar mass-to-light ratios are nuisance assumptions. They are not direct measurements of dark matter.

What SPARC contributes

SPARC is valuable because it combines homogeneous 3.6 μm photometry, resolved rotation curves and published Newtonian gas, disc and bulge contributions. In this project I preserve those source components rather than replacing every galaxy with the same toy disk.

Where the uncertainties are

Distance changes the physical radius scale; inclination changes the deprojected speed; non-circular motions can bias individual radii; stellar populations change Υ. Cored haloes, NFW haloes and phenomenological acceleration mappings can therefore be difficult to distinguish over a limited radial range.

My interpretation

I do not use a single galaxy to declare one halo profile “correct.” I look for residual structure, posterior covariance, population regularities and consistency with evidence at other scales. Often the interesting result is not the smallest χ² but the assumption that must move to obtain it.

Future experimental outlook

Better distances, resolved gas kinematics, stellar-population constraints and multi-tracer dynamics can tighten the problem. The long-term target is hierarchical inference across the population with nuisance parameters propagated instead of fixed.
